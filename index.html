⇅ Getting a client ID

Google needs to know this app is yours before it lets it use your Drive. It's free and takes about ten minutes, once.

  1. Open console.cloud.google.com and sign in with the Google account whose Drive you want to use. Make a new project — call it “Scriptorium”.
  2. In APIs & Services → Library, find Google Drive API and press Enable.
  3. In APIs & Services → OAuth consent screen, choose External. Give it a name and your email. Under Scopes, add …/auth/drive.appdata. Under Test users, add your own Gmail address. Leave it in Testing.
  4. In APIs & Services → Credentials, choose Create credentials → OAuth client ID → Web application. Under Authorized JavaScript origins, add the address this app lives at — just the start, like https://yourname.github.io (and later your own domain too).
  5. Copy the Client ID it shows you, paste it into the box in Settings, and press Connect Google Drive.

The app only asks for its own hidden folder in your Drive — it can't see or touch any of your other files. Do the same Connect step once on each device.

Write plainly — or use these little marks, and the Read view turns them into a finished page. The marks stay in Write; the magic shows in Read.

Tap ⋯ on the formatting bar for lists, checklists, links, tables and more — or type the marks yourself.

📚 How the bookshelf works

The bookshelf keeps picture books, PDFs and illustrated tales — read under the stars. Any file you bring in finds its own place: writing becomes sheets, pictures become books.

📚 Add books (or New → Bring in what you have) takes .cbz archives, .pdf files, or a handful of images. Choose several PDFs at once and the merge bench opens — drag rows by the ⠿ grip into order; pages are copied whole (perfect) or pressed toward email size, with filled-in forms pressed flat.

🧵 Bind joins anything already on the shelf, whatever it began as — a PDF with a picture book with a bundle of photos. Tap books in order, name the volume, done.

PDFs keep their words. In the reader, ⋯ → Find in this book searches every page, and Select & highlight text lets you mark passages in five colors. Searching the shelf finds words inside PDFs too. A locked PDF asks for its password once; after that it opens freely, and downloading it gives a copy without the lock.

Hold a book (or right-click) for its quick menu — open, rename, download, remove.

In the reader, swipe or tap the edges to turn pages. The ⋯ menu sets which way pages turn and how they fit. Your place is always remembered.

Books live only in this browser — the originals on your device are never touched.

🔍 Read the text in

For scanned books — photos of pages, where there's no text to search yet. The app reads every page and adds the words invisibly, so you can find, select, highlight and quote them.

The first time, the reading tool and each language are downloaded once (the tool about 5 MB, each language roughly 2–15 MB), then it all works offline. It takes a few seconds per page, and you can stop any time — it picks up where it left off.
